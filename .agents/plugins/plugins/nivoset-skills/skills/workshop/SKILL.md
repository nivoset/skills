# Workshop

Turn an early idea into a decision-ready plan through user-led gates and collaboration among relevant specialists. This skill extends Blackboard with a product-led sequence. Use Blackboard for board state, evidence, stable IDs, authority, disagreement handling, and readiness; do not replace its safeguards with role consensus.

## Boundary

This is a planning workflow. Prepare product direction, architecture and infrastructure plans, system connections, contracts, capability and feature documents, dependencies, acceptance behaviors, and review findings. Do not write or change application code or perform product implementation. Implementation-ticket drafts require a separate explicit request. Store planning artifacts in the workshop folder and its subfolders, and link related documents by relative path. Give each document one clear concern: split distinct decisions, contracts, capabilities, or processes into separate files, using forms such as ADRs where appropriate. Keep documents short to medium length and no longer than 10 rendered pages; split a document when it combines concerns or exceeds that limit. Check Markdown and Mermaid, and validate applicable Gherkin feature files and OpenAPI contracts before calling documents reviewable. Planning validation does not include implementing or executing product work.

## Workflow

1. **Initial setup.** Talk with the user to establish the desired outcome, affected users, success measures, scope and non-goals, constraints, evidence, assumptions, and known unknowns. Inspect available evidence before asking questions. Record material claims, evidence, assumptions, and open decisions on the Blackboard. Keep this setup stage user-led; begin product-specialist work after the user passes the setup gate. Surface any decision that could cause major rework if changed later as an explicit issue with options, evidence, impact, and the decision owner. The user decides when this setup gate is passed; do not treat expert consensus as approval.
2. **Parallel product work.** After the user passes the setup gate, have product specialists develop and challenge product direction in parallel. Assign distinct, bounded areas such as outcome and scope, user value, viability, or assumptions and evaluation, selecting only areas relevant to the idea. They should communicate across overlapping areas and reconcile their recommendations with evidence. Experts may decide details that are easy to change within a sprint or two and do not alter product outcomes, scope, success measures, risk, infrastructure, or system connections. Bring other decisions to the user as issues; do not silently settle them. Once recommendations and outstanding issues are clear, present the product gate and wait for the user to pass it before starting engineering planning.
3. **Engineering collaboration.** After the product gate, assign relevant engineering and architecture specialists to define architecture, system connections, infrastructure, data and interface contracts, dependencies, and operational constraints. They may discuss contracts and sources directly with product specialists and send product-impacting questions back for resolution. Trace technical choices to outcomes or explicit constraints. Surface choices involving major rework, infrastructure, integrations, material cost or risk, or changed user behavior to the user for sign-off. Experts may resolve only easily replaceable details as defined above. Present the architecture and technical-plan gate to the user; proceed to documentation after the user passes it.
4. **Documentation preparation.** Convert user-approved product direction and technical plans into focused, linked Markdown documents under the workshop folder and its subfolders rather than one large document. Keep each file to one concern: a decision, contract, capability, feature behavior, process, or other coherent topic. Use separate ADRs for distinct architectural decisions. Group related files in directories and link each document to its source and prerequisite documents. Use Mermaid for architecture and dependency diagrams where useful, Gherkin for feature behavior where applicable, and OpenAPI for HTTP service contracts where applicable. Keep documents short to medium length and below 10 rendered pages; split mixed or oversized documents along clear concern boundaries. Do not create implementation tickets unless separately requested.
5. **Review and readiness.** Review the document set for missing, conflicting, unsafe, unclear, or unsupported material. Route findings to their owners, preserve dissent and provenance, revise affected documents, and re-review them. Run available lint and structural checks for Markdown, Mermaid, Gherkin, and OpenAPI artifacts that are present. Report failed or unavailable checks rather than calling the affected documents reviewable. Run Blackboard's post-draft completeness pass. The user decides whether the documentation gate is passed; until then, report open issues and do not claim final readiness.
6. **Report status.** Return the document index with links, current product direction, architecture and technical plan, contracts, feature preparation, dependencies, check results, assumptions, and Blackboard readiness. Distinguish user-approved decisions from expert recommendations. For each unresolved major decision, show its owner, affected IDs and documents, evidence, options, impact, and re-entry condition.

## Team and handoffs

Read [references/team-model.md](references/team-model.md) before the first expert dispatch. The product, engineering, and documentation stages follow user-controlled gates. Parallelize work within a stage when questions are independent; if new evidence affects an earlier stage, bring it back to that stage and flag whether the relevant gate needs to be revisited. Experts propose evidence and recommendations; the main agent maintains the board and integrates contributions. Model choice never implies additional decision authority.

## Blackboard dependency

Use the `blackboard` skill and its current board contract when available. Follow its roster announcement, dispatch records, contribution validation, freshness checks, disagreement rooms, implication sweeps, and readiness rules. If Blackboard or delegated-agent capability is unavailable, say which part cannot be performed and keep the work at the level that can be supported; do not simulate agent dispatches or claim readiness that depends on missing reviews.
